# Write-up

During the 5-second test, all five participants immediately understood the purpose of the application: Tiffany recognized that it helps students find safe rides, Elbert identified it as a UC Davis SafeRide booking app, Gabe stated it was a SafeRide application, Jessica noted the title and stated it helps students book a SafeRide, and Navya identified it as a free transportation service for UC Davis students and faculty. When completing the scenario task of requesting a ride from Kemper Hall to The U apartments, user navigation varied based on interface clarity and remote testing conditions: Elbert navigated through the sign-in and booking sequence without getting stuck, Gabe found locating Kemper Hall on the map slightly confusing at first before using the search option, Jessica encountered interface roadblocks due to Zoom display cropping that cut off top navigation buttons and struggled briefly to select her pickup spot, and Navya completed the entire process effortlessly without getting stuck. In the debriefs, difficulty ratings ranged between 1 and 2 on a 5-point scale. Tiffany rated the difficulty at 1/5 with no changes suggested and described it as a safe ride app that lets students find rides home late at night. Elbert rated it at 1/5, asked for clarification regarding the standalone numbers on the map, and described it as a tool to help request safe rides on campus late at night. Gabe rated difficulty at 2/5, suggested adding location search history, and described it as a helpful alternative to Uber for UC Davis students. Jessica rated difficulty at 1.5/5, suggested adding labels to explain map numbers, and described it as a quick and convenient safe ride app. And Navya rated difficulty at 1/5, suggested satellite map views and live ride tracking, and described it as an application for booking rides from campus to off-campus locations.

The primary pattern of confusion across users centered on the map display, where isolated numbers like 1, 2, and 4 appeared without context, causing multiple participants, including Elbert, Jessica, and Gabe, to get confused about what those figures represented until it was explained that they signified van numbers. However, a strong positive pattern was how "straightforward" the app was, with every participant rating the app between 1 and 2 for difficulty and agreeing that the booking flow was clear and intuitive once initiated. Any minor problemss were technical, e.g. screen cropping issues for Jessica when using Zoom to test the app.

Based on these findings, the principal design modification will be replacing the plain text numbers on the map display with clear van icons so users instantly recognize active rides. I would add a driver communication option like an in-app messaging feature to communicate directly with drivers would significantly improve the user experience when trying to locate a ride late at night, though there was insufficient time to implement this functionality in the current prototype. This user testing phase validated that the ride request flow is straightforward and accessible, while highlighting visual map updates and communication tools needed for the next app version.
